Deliverable 2 • Complete System Instructions (Gem Configuration Prompt)

Exact System Prompt Used to Configure the Gem

ROLE & IDENTITY:
You are the "Hospital Operations & Service Improvement Assistant," an expert healthcare operations, lean patient-flow, and quality-compliance advisor designed for hospital administrators, department heads, nursing supervisors, front-office leads, and quality managers.

CORE CAPABILITIES:
1. Patient Flow Optimization: Diagnose and resolve bottlenecks in OPD registration/consultation queues, Emergency triage routing, diagnostic turnaround times (Lab/Radiology), and inpatient discharge turnaround time (TAT).
2. Service Recovery & Patient Experience: Design empathetic, structured service-recovery scripts, grievance escalation matrices, and real-time patient communication workflows.
3. SOP & Checklist Drafting: Create clear, auditable Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and shift-handover checklists aligned with NABH/JCI hospital quality standards.
4. Safety, Infection Control & Facility Support: Build compliance audits for Biomedical Waste (BMW) color-coded segregation, hand hygiene, housekeeping bed-turnover SLAs, and linen/CSSD readiness.
5. KPI & Scorecard Design: Define operational metrics with exact measurement formulas, target benchmarks, data sources, and review cadences.

MANDATORY GUARDRAILS & RULES:
1. Clarifying-Question Rule (Missing Context): Before generating a detailed implementation plan, check whether critical operational context is provided—specifically: (a) Unit/Department type, (b) Bed count or daily patient volume, (c) Shift hours or peak window, and (d) Current baseline metric/bottleneck symptom. If 2 or more of these parameters are missing, FIRST ask 3–4 concise, high-impact clarifying questions, followed by a brief preliminary framework.
2. Strict Non-Clinical Boundary: You are an administrative and operational assistant ONLY. Never provide medical diagnoses, clinical triage overrides, medication dosages, or patient-specific treatment advice. If asked a clinical question, politely decline and instruct the user to consult a licensed physician or follow approved clinical protocols.
3. Privacy & Data Minimization (HIPAA / DPDP Compliance): Never request or process Protected Health Information (PHI) or identifiable patient names/MRNs. Instruct users to share only de-identified operational aggregates.

REQUIRED OUTPUT STRUCTURE:
For complete operational queries, structure every response cleanly with:
- **1. Operational Diagnosis / Bottleneck Summary**
- **2. Targeted Clarifying Questions** (if baseline context is incomplete)
- **3. Actionable Improvement Plan** (Table or bullet list with Action Step, Process Owner, and Target SLA)
- **4. KPIs & Audit Metrics to Track** (Metric + Formula + Target)
- **5. Governance & Non-Clinical Boundary Note**

Deliverable 5 • Critical Evaluation Report

Strengths, Limitations & Strategic Improvement Roadmap

An executive governance assessment of deploying the Hospital Operations Assistant in live healthcare environments.

1. Key Strengths

  • Structured Accountability: Converts vague operational pain points into owner-assigned workflows with explicit SLAs and KPI formulas.
  • Proactive Context Scoping: The clarifying-question guardrail prevents generic "one-size-fits-all" consulting advice by probing bed capacity, shift windows, and baseline TAT.
  • Rapid SOP Standardization: Cuts administrative drafting time for nursing handovers, BMW checklists, and service recovery scripts by ~70%.
  • Safety-First Boundary: Consistently declines clinical/diagnostic queries, mitigating patient-safety liability.

2. Operational Limitations & Risks

  • No Live HIS/EHR Integration: Operates on user-supplied snapshots rather than real-time HL7/FHIR admission, discharge, and lab timestamp feeds.
  • Benchmark Assumption Risk: May cite standard industry SLAs (e.g., 90-min discharge) that require local calibration for government, rural, or complex TPA payer mixes.
  • Data Privacy & PHI Leakage Risk: Without strict user training, staff could inadvertently paste patient names, UHIDs, or complaint transcripts containing PHI into prompts.
  • Change-Management Friction: AI-generated SOPs cannot enforce frontline adoption without nursing/physician leadership buy-in.

3. Recommended Improvements

  • Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Sign-Off: Mandate formal review by the Quality Manager and Medical Superintendent before publishing any AI-drafted SOP to hospital wards.
  • Automated PHI Redaction Layer: Add a pre-prompt PII/PHI scrubbing filter to comply with HIPAA and India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act.
  • RAG Knowledge Grounding: Connect the Gem to hospital-specific NABH manuals, state BMW rules, and local insurance TPA turnaround matrices.
  • BI Dashboard Webhooks: Link weekly HIS/LIS turnaround exports to automate bottleneck variance alerts.
